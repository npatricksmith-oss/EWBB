# Pitcher Animation Reference: Windup vs. Stretch Mechanics

This report provides biomechanical, positional, and timing reference data for animating a right-handed pitcher (RHP) in a retro baseball game remake of *Earl Weaver Baseball* (1987). It covers both behind-the-plate/center-field view and isometric field perspective.

## 2. Key Differences Between Windup and Stretch

| Dimension | Windup | Stretch (Set Position) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Primary Usage** | Bases empty (allows full rotational momentum and timing rhythm). | Runners on base (to control the running game and prevent steals) or full-time by modern relievers. |
| **Starting Orientation** | Facing home plate (0°). Chest, belt buckle, and toes point toward batter. | Facing 3rd base (90° to plate for RHP). Body profile presented to home plate. |
| **Footwork Before Lift** | Multi-step sequence: Rocker step backward with free foot, followed by pivot foot turning 90° against rubber. | Static: Pivot foot starts already positioned parallel and flush to the front edge of the rubber. Zero preliminary steps. |
| **Upper Body Movement** | Broad preliminary motion: Hands often swing overhead past head/crown before returning to chest. | Compact: Hands come together at face/chest and descend to a set point at belt or chest. |
| **Legal Stop Requirement** | None. Motion must be smooth and continuous without pause once initiated (Rule 5.07(a)(1)). | **Mandatory complete stop** with perceptible pause before leg lift begins (Rule 5.07(a)(2)). Penalty: Balk. |
| **Leg Lift Height & Coiling** | Maximum height (thigh parallel or well above parallel, 90°–110° hip flexion; hips often coil 10°–20° toward 2B). | Moderate height (hip parallel, 70°–90° flexion) or flat "slide step" (<30° flexion) to expedite time to plate. |
| **Delivery Time (Lift to Plate)** | ~1.40 s – 1.80 s (total time from initial rocker to plate: 2.20 s – 2.80 s). | ~1.20 s – 1.35 s standard; ~1.00 s – 1.15 s with slide step. |

## 3. Concrete Biomechanical Breakdown by Rule & Checklist

### 1. Starting Stance
* **Windup:** The pitcher stands squarely atop the pitching rubber, chest facing home plate (0°). For an RHP, the right (pivot) foot is positioned flat on top of the rubber, angled between 0° and 30° toward the first-base line. The left (free) foot rests slightly beside or on a line with the front edge of the rubber. Feet are shoulder-width apart (14–18 inches). Knees maintain a soft 5°–10° bend. The torso is erect with a slight 5° forward lean.
* **Stretch:** The pitcher stands sideways. The right (pivot) foot is flush against and parallel to the front edge of the rubber, pointing along the 3B foul line (90° relative to home plate). The left foot is placed 14–20 inches forward down the mound slope toward home plate, roughly shoulder-width apart, pointing between 3B and home (45°–90° angle). The torso faces 3B (90°). Knees are bent 10°–15°, and weight is distributed roughly 60% on the back foot and 40% on the front foot.

### 2. Taking the Sign
* **Windup:** Pitcher stands facing the plate. Hands are held together at chest height, or the glove rests against the thigh while the bare throwing hand dangles at the right hip or holds the ball behind the right hip/back pocket. The head looks straight toward the catcher.
* **Stretch (Rule 5.07(a)(2) & Rule 5.07(f)):** The pitcher **must** take the sign with the pivot foot in contact with the rubber and **hands separated**. Typically, the glove rests on the front (left) knee or hip, and the bare hand hangs loose at the right thigh or behind the right hip. The head is turned 80°–90° over the left shoulder, looking in toward the catcher’s signals.

### 3. Hand Set / "Come Set" & The Full Stop
* **Setting the Hands:** In the stretch, the pitcher initiates "the stretch" preliminary movement by bringing the hands together from a separated position, gathering them at chin, chest, or belt level.
* **Full Stop (MLB Rule 5.07(a)(2)):** The pitcher must hold the ball in both hands in front of the body and come to a **complete, recognizable stop**. The rule states:
  > *"Preparatory to making an artful stretch, the pitcher must hold the ball in both hands in front of his body and come to a complete stop. This must be sufficient to establish a perceptible pause before the delivery begins."*
* **Stop Duration:** Umpires look for a perceptible freeze of all motion (hands, shoulders, knees) lasting typically **0.50 s to 1.00 s** (never a rolling motion or "hesitation twitch"). If any runner is on base, failure to achieve a complete stop is ruled an immediate **balk** (Rule 6.02(a)(13)), advancing all runners one base.

### 4. Step Sequence for the Windup
* **Rocker Step:** The pitcher initiates motion with a small step backward (4–8 inches) using the left (free) foot. Direction is either straight back toward 2nd base (180°) or angled slightly (45°) toward shortstop/3rd base. A step longer than 8–10 inches pulls the head off-line.
* **Pivot Foot Slide:** As weight shifts momentarily to the left foot, the right (pivot) foot slides across the rubber surface and rotates 90° clockwise, locking parallel and flush against the front vertical edge of the rubber (pointing down the 3B line). Weight shifts 100% back over the right heel/instep.
* **Hands: Classic Full Windup vs. Modern/No-Windup:**
  * *Classic Full Windup (1970s–1980s, e.g., Nolan Ryan, Fernando Valenzuela):* Hands travel from chest level up over the head, extending arms nearly straight above the crown of the head as the rocker step finishes, creating maximum rhythmic flow before descending back to chest height during leg lift.
  * *Modern / Compact Windup (e.g., Greg Maddux, Justin Verlander):* Hands remain pinned at chest or chin level; no overhead extension occurs.

### 5. Leg Lift & Balance Point
* **Knee Height:** In a standard windup, the lead knee drives up to belt or chest height, reaching an angle of **90° to 110° hip flexion** (roughly 60%–70% of standing height). In the stretch, standard knee lift is slightly lower (waist/chest, 70°–90°).
* **Coiling / Tuck toward 2B:** The front hip rotates closed, turning the lead knee 10°–20° toward 2nd base. This "coils" the glute and pelvis around the back hip socket.
* **Balance Point:** A momentary posture where the pitcher's weight is balanced over the instep of the bent rear leg (R knee flexed ~15°–25°), head directly over the pelvis, eyes locked over the lead shoulder toward home plate.
* **Slide Step Variation (Stretch with Base Stealer):** The pitcher does not elevate the front knee toward the waist. Instead, the left knee elevates at most 4–8 inches (hip flexion <30°), keeping the front foot low to the ground and driving immediately toward the plate. This shaves **0.20–0.30 seconds** off delivery time, but reduces lower-half rotational force.

### 6. Leg Lift to Ball Release
* **Stride Length & Direction:** The front leg strides linearly down the mound toward home plate. Stride length averages **75% to 85% of body height** (5.0 to 5.5 feet for a 6'0" pitcher). The foot lands within 2 to 6 inches of the direct center line connecting the rubber to home plate.
* **Hand Break:** As the front hip begins translating down the mound from the balance point, hands break at chest level. The fingers stay on top of the ball with wrists firm, thumbs rotating downward toward the thighs ("thumb-to-thigh").
* **Arm Swing:** The throwing arm draws down, back, and up in an elliptical loop. By front foot contact, the throwing elbow is raised to shoulder level (abducted ~90°), and the forearm is vertical or cocked back.
* **Glove Arm:** Points forward toward the target during stride, then pulls firmly into the left ribs/chest ("tucking the glove") to establish a rotational anchor point.
* **Hip-Shoulder Separation:** At front foot strike, the pelvis has already rotated open 60°–80° toward home plate, while the shoulder axis remains closed (facing 3B). This creates **30° to 45° of angular separation**, stretching the core musculature to whip the upper torso through.
* **Front Foot Landing:** Left foot lands firmly at an angle of 0° to 15° closed (pointing slightly toward the right-handed batter's box). The knee hits at ~45° flexion, then forcefully extends ("blocks") to halt forward movement and transfer kinetic energy upward.
* **Max External Rotation (MER / Layback):** As the torso rotates forward, the throwing shoulder externally rotates back to an extreme angle of **160° to 180°**, placing maximum stretch on the anterior shoulder and elbow joint.
* **Release Point:** Shoulder rapidly internally rotates at 7,000°–9,000°/s. Ball release occurs 8–12 inches in front of the front knee, with forward trunk tilt of 30°–45°.
* **Follow-Through & Fielding Base:** The throwing arm sweeps across the body, finishing near the left shin/hip. The right rear leg unweights, swings forward in an arc, and lands square with the left foot, placing the pitcher in a wide, balanced fielding stance facing the batter with the glove chest-high.

### 7. Quantitative Delivery Timing
* **Windup Delivery Duration:** **2.00 s to 2.80 s** from initial rocker step initiation to ball release.
* **Leg Lift Apex to Ball Release:** **0.90 s to 1.20 s**.
* **Foot Contact to Ball Release:** **0.145 s (145 milliseconds)** (ASMI empirical standard).
* **Arm Acceleration (MER to Release):** **0.050 s (50 milliseconds)**.
* **Stretch Delivery Time to Plate (Standard Leg Lift):** **1.25 s to 1.45 s** from first movement of the front leg until ball arrives in catcher’s mitt.
* **Stretch Delivery Time to Plate (Slide Step):** **1.00 s to 1.15 s** to plate.
* **Scouting Benchmark:** MLB benchmark for controlling the running game is **1.30 s or less** from first forward movement to the catcher’s glove. Above 1.40 s invites base-stealing jumps.

### 8. Tactical Application: Windup vs. Stretch
* **Windup:** Used with **bases empty**, or occasionally with a runner solely on third base (where a steal of home on the windup is unlikely). It allows maximum kinetic gathering and personal tempo rhythm.
* **Stretch:** Mandatory with **runners on first, second, or bases loaded**. Eliminates the slow rocker step to allow rapid pickoff throws to bases (Rule 5.07(a)(2)).
* **Modern Trend:** Many modern relievers (and several elite starters including Shohei Ohtani, Spencer Strider, Carlos Carrasco, and Stephen Strasburg) throw **100% from the stretch** in all game states to reduce mechanical variables, preserve repeatable arm slots, and simplify training to a single motor pattern.


### Key Takeaway for Retro Sprite Engines
When drawing small sprites (16x16 to 32x32 pixels) from a center-field or isometric perspective:
1. **Pose 1 (The Set):** Clear vertical silhouette; hands stationary at chest/belt.
2. **Pose 2 (Apex Leg Lift):** Lead knee pulled up to horizontal, foot dangling straight down. For isometric view, rotate hips 15° toward 2B.
3. **Pose 3 (Stride / Power T):** Legs wide apart (80% of sprite height), throwing arm bent upward 90° at elbow, glove arm pointing forward.
4. **Pose 4 (Release):** Torso leaned forward 40°, throwing arm fully extended toward plate, ball detached from hand.
5. **Pose 5 (Fielding):** Sprite lands with feet square, knees bent, glove forward.

### Biomechanics Literature & Official Rules

1. **American Sports Medicine Institute (ASMI) / Dr. Glenn Fleisig & Dr. James Andrews:**
   * *Kinematics of Baseball Pitching:* Defines the 6 core pitching delivery phases: Windup, Stride, Arm Cocking, Arm Acceleration, Arm Deceleration, and Follow-Through.
   * *Verified Timing Metric:* Foot contact to ball release = **145 milliseconds (0.145 s)**; acceleration phase = **50 milliseconds (0.050 s)**.
   * *URL:* [https://www.asmi.org/research/](https://www.asmi.org/research/)
2. **MLB Official Baseball Rules (Rule 5.07 - Pitching):**
   * *Rule 5.07(a)(1) (The Windup Position):* Pivot foot on or in front of rubber, free foot on or behind rubber. One step back, one step forward. Continuous motion without stop.
