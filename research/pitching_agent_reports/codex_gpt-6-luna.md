# Pitcher start positions: windup vs. stretch

Times below cover the movement **from the first delivery motion to ball release**; they exclude the time spent taking signs or waiting on the mound. Pose details describe a typical right-handed overhand pitcher. Individual deliveries vary, so use them as sprite reference points rather than rules every pitcher must match.

## Windup

| Phase | Approx. time | Feet | Legs | Torso facing | Hands/glove | Throwing arm | Head |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Take sign / starting stance** | Variable; not part of delivery | Right pivot foot contacts rubber. Left foot may be on, in front of, behind, or beside rubber. A common stance has feet about shoulder-width apart. | Relaxed, slight knee bend; weight slightly favoring right/back leg. | Shoulders and hips generally face home plate. | Hands can rest together at chest or lower torso, or separately; ball can be in glove or throwing hand. | Relaxed at side or gathered with glove. | Eyes toward catcher’s target. |
| **Rocker / initial movement** | Unverified as a general duration | Left stride foot takes a short step back or to the side; then right pivot foot turns so its outside edge is near the front edge of rubber, roughly parallel to it. | Weight shifts back; knees remain soft. | Begins square to plate, then coils/turns slightly toward first-base side. | Hands move together or rise; placement varies from chest to head. | Usually still gathered or beginning to move. | Stays level, eyes on target. |
| **Leg lift / balance** | Windup phase ends at maximum knee height; exact duration varies by pitcher | Right foot remains pivoting/contacting rubber; left foot lifts. | Left thigh rises, commonly toward belt or chest height; knee bent about 90° or more. Some pitchers lift lower. | Slightly turned away from plate; avoid portraying a full back-to-plate turn as universal. | Hands commonly together at chest, chin, or above head in a classic windup. Modern compact motions often keep them at chest. | Hands separate as stride begins; right arm moves down and back. | Head centered over pivot-foot side, eyes remain on target. |
| **Stride / arm cocking** | Timing varies; no single general value verified | Left foot strides toward plate, usually near a straight line from rubber to home; stride length varies. Research summaries report ranges around 66–100% of body height, depending on study and method. | Right leg drives; left knee flexes on landing. | Hips begin rotating toward plate before shoulders; shoulders remain relatively closed early, creating separation. | Hands break; glove arm reaches toward target and then firms/folds inward. | Right arm swings down, back, then up; at foot contact it is cocked, elbow near shoulder height. | Level, moving toward target. |
| **Release / follow-through** | Overall windup motion-to-release duration: **unverified as a universal range** | Left foot lands slightly closed to target line; right leg trails, drags, then comes through. | Front knee flexes to absorb force, then firms; torso folds forward. | Rotates fully toward plate. | Glove arm folds toward torso/hip. | Accelerates overhand and releases in front of the throwing shoulder; then decelerates across body. | Moves over stride knee; pitcher finishes balanced and ready to field. |

## Stretch (set position)

| Phase | Approx. time | Feet | Legs | Torso facing | Hands/glove | Throwing arm | Head |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Take sign / initial stance** | Variable; not part of delivery | Right pivot foot contacts and is parallel to rubber; left foot is in front. A coaching guide recommends feet about shoulder-width apart. | Slight bend; weight back over flexed right leg. | For a right-hander, shoulders turn toward third-base side to some degree; glove-side shoulder points toward plate. | Before coming set, one hand is at the side; ball may be concealed in glove. | Relaxed at side or near glove. | Eyes may look to runners, then target. |
| **Come set / stop** | Rule requires a complete stop with runners aboard; **no fixed one-second minimum in MLB rules** | Feet stationary in set stance. | Balanced over rubber, knees soft. | Partly sideways, not square to plate. | Both hands together in front of body, commonly between chest and navel. | Ball concealed in glove. | Eyes level on catcher/target. |
| **Leg lift / slide step** | College coaching goal: **1.30 seconds or less from stretch delivery to release**, not a universal MLB average. Slide step can shorten it; a universal slide-step time is unverified. | Right foot pushes from rubber; left foot lifts or slides directly toward plate. | Full motion may lift left knee near belt height; slide step uses a much shorter lift, often only enough to clear the ground. | Begins partly side-on; hips then rotate toward plate. | Hands break before or as left leg moves; Washington State’s manual specifically teaches separation before leg lift for a quick delivery. | Right hand leaves glove and moves down/back. | Turns eyes/head to plate and stays level. |
| **Stride / arm cocking** | Included in the motion-to-release interval above. | Left foot strides toward plate; avoid drawing it toward first or third as the default. | Right leg drives; left knee flexes at landing. | Hips open before shoulders; shoulders stay closed briefly, then rotate. | Glove arm points toward target, then folds inward. | Arm travels down, back, and up into cocked position. | Stays on line to target. |
| **Release / follow-through** | Included in motion-to-release interval above. | Left foot lands slightly closed; right foot drags and comes through. | Front leg braces without locking; torso folds forward. | Rotates toward plate. | Glove folds inward/down. | Releases in front of shoulder, follows across body. | Finishes over stride knee; balanced fielding position. |

## Key differences

- **Starting orientation:** In the windup, the pitcher faces the batter to some degree. In the set position, a right-hander is more side-on, with the glove-side shoulder toward home and the throwing shoulder toward center field. These are definitions of the positions, not claims that every pitcher stands at an exact angle.
- **Feet:** In the windup the free left foot has several legal starting locations. In the set position it must be in front of the rubber, while the pivot foot contacts it.
- **Windup sequence:** A common version adds a short left-foot rocker step, then turns the right pivot foot and lifts the left leg. The step may be back or lateral; it is not always a backward step toward second base. Hands may rise over the head in a classic full windup, but chest-height or other compact hand positions are also common.
- **Stretch sequence:** After the set stop, the pitcher lifts the left leg or uses a slide step. The preliminary arm-and-body motion sometimes called “the stretch” happens **before** the required set position.
- **Pause:** With runners on base, MLB Rule 5.07 requires the pitcher to hold the ball in both hands in front of the body and come to a complete stop. The rule does not specify a one-second minimum. A Washington State coaching manual suggests varying holds and describes a quick-tempo pause of approximately one second.
- **Timing:** The Washington State manual’s 1.30-second target is from stretch delivery to release, with command. It is a coaching target intended to help against steals, not a published MLB average and not the time including the hold. A single typical windup duration, or a substantiated universal slide-step range of 1.0–1.2 seconds, is **unverified** in the sources reviewed.
- **When used:** Windup is commonly used with bases empty; set is commonly used with runners aboard to shorten delivery and help control the running game. Either position may be used at any time under MLB rules. MLB notes that many relievers use set position even with bases empty.
- **Body finish:** Both deliveries converge on the same broad sequence: stride, arm cocking, release, and follow-through. The windup adds preliminary movement; the stretch shortens that lead-in. The pitcher typically finishes leaning over the stride leg, with the pivot leg coming through, ready to field.

## Retro game reference

It does **not** document the pitcher’s windup and stretch as a specific frame count or give pose-by-pose screenshots, so the exact poses and number of unique pitcher frames are **unverified** from that source.
- An NES *R.B.I. Baseball* screenshot archive is available, but it does not establish a windup-versus-stretch animation breakdown or frame count. That detail is **unverified**.
- For a compact retro sprite, the minimum recognizable distinction is the **starting pose and transition**: windup begins more front-facing and may include a rocker/foot pivot; stretch begins side-on with both hands together at chest-to-belt height and a visible pause, then a short lift or slide step.

## Sources and what they support

- [MLB Official Baseball Rules, Rule 5.07](https://img.mlbstatic.com/mlb-images/image/upload/mlb/wqn5ah4c3qtivwx3jatm.pdf) — Legal windup and set foot positions, free-foot options in the windup, requirement to stop in the set with runners aboard, and distinction between preliminary stretch and set position.
- [MLB: Windup Position](https://www.mlb.com/glossary/rules/windup-position) — Plain-language position and sequence: pivot foot on rubber, shoulders toward batter, optional step back or side, pivot-foot turn, lift and stride.
- [MLB: Set Position](https://www.mlb.com/glossary/rules/set-position) — Right-hander’s side-on orientation, hands in front, delivery and slide-step description, common use with runners, and note that many relievers stay in set position.
- [Baseball Canada: Pitching Mechanics](https://www.baseball.ca/?alias=pitching-mechanics&lang=english) — Coaching descriptions of stance, short windup step, hand-height options, pivot, knee lift, set stance, hands at chest-to-belly-button, hand break, arm path, release and fielding-ready finish. This is a youth coaching reference, so treat its measurements as teaching cues, not universal MLB norms.
- [Washington State University Baseball Pitching Manual](https://cdn1.sportngin.com/attachments/document/802c-3330691/Pitching_Manual_-_Final.pdf) — Right-handed stretch delivery target of 1.30 seconds or less, modified lift or slide step, hand separation before leg lift, and variable set-to-delivery holds.
- [A Clinician’s Guide to Analysis of the Pitching Motion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6542879/) — Biomechanical phase names and reported stride-length ranges; these vary across the cited studies.
- [The Clinician’s Guide to Baseball Pitching Biomechanics](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9950989/) — Phase landmarks from first motion through maximum knee height, foot contact, maximum shoulder external rotation, release and follow-through.
- [MLB Tonight: Windup and stretch](https://www.mlb.com/video/mlb-tonight-windup-and-stretch) — Video explanation by Al Leiter and Ryan Dempster of the two deliveries.
- [Yoshinobu Yamamoto slow-motion windup, side view](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mT7hArbHhhg) — Slow-motion live pitcher reference from the first-base side.
- [Zack Wheeler slow-motion windup, third-base side view](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XS1Zh1-nfgU) — Slow-motion live pitcher reference from the third-base side.
- [SABR Gaming and Simulations: *Earl Weaver Baseball* review](https://sabrbaseballgaming.com/2023/08/05/retro-computer-baseball-game-review-earl-weaver-baseball-2/) — Reports fluid player animation and frame-by-frame instant replay, but does not quantify pitcher frames or compare windup and stretch poses.
- [MobyGames: *R.B.I. Baseball* screenshots](https://www.mobygames.com/game/10371/rbi-baseball/screenshots/) — Screenshot archive for a related retro game; it does not provide an animation frame count.
