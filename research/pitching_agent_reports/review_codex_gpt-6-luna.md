## Windup — Nolan Ryan

Ranked by impact on realism:

1. **Torso never coils to show the back.** At **0.23–0.36**, the game keeps Ryan’s chest facing the viewer as he lifts his knee. In the Ryan and Darvish references, he turns side-on and then coils far enough that the viewer sees his back and jersey number. **Fix:** rotate the shoulders and hips away from the plate during the lift, with the back visible near the peak.

2. **The hands rise too early and stay overhead too long.** At **0.03–0.17**, the game already has both hands above the head. The Ryan reference shows the lift developing over a longer sequence, with the hands rising as the body turns. **Fix:** keep the hands lower at the start, then raise them in time with the turn and knee lift.

3. **No clear rocker step or pivot turn.** At **0.00–0.20**, the feet stay nearly planted and parallel. The reference shows a small step back with the glove-side foot, then the pivot foot turning as the pitcher moves into the coil. **Fix:** add a short backward rocker step and turn the pivot foot toward the side-on slot.

4. **Throwing arm appears on the wrong side.** Around **0.36–0.42**, the ball/throwing arm is on the viewer’s right; for this right-handed pitcher it should be on the viewer’s left. **Fix:** swap the arm and ball placement.

## Stretch — Ryan and Quisenberry

1. **Starting stance is too square.** At **0.00**, both sprites face the viewer with feet spread. The Wacha reference shows a side-on stance, chest facing toward third base. **Fix:** turn the torso and feet side-on before taking the sign.

2. **The lift gets too high and drifts out of the front.** Ryan at **0.23–0.28** and Quisenberry at **0.23–0.30** lift the knee toward chest height; later frames continue into a high kick. The stretch reference keeps the lift lower and in front. **Fix:** raise the knee only to about belt height and keep it toward the plate.

3. **The set needs a clearer pause.** At **0.025–0.23**, the hands are together at the waist through repeated frames, but the body remains upright and square. Wacha’s hands come together at the belt in a side-on, settled position before the lift. **Fix:** add a still set pose after bringing the hands together.
